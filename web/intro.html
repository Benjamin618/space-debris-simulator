Project Introduction

Why This Sandbox Exists

Intro Live Demo Analysis Results

This application is a compact engineering sandbox for LEO proximity tracking: a radar observes nearby debris, a Kalman tracker estimates their motion, and the web app shows both the live behavior and the batch evidence behind the conclusions.

The goal is not to reproduce orbital mechanics perfectly. The goal is to make sensing, tracking, and estimator mismatch visible enough that an external reader can understand the trade-offs.

Scenario in One Minute

Imagine a servicing or debris-collection spacecraft already in proximity operations, not searching all of LEO at large scale. It uses a local radar to repeatedly scan a small neighborhood, detect objects, and maintain tracks good enough to support decisions.

In the sandbox, truth motion is bounded second-order, while the estimator stays first-order constant velocity. That deliberate mismatch is what makes the batch interesting: it shows when the tracker remains usable and when it starts to drift.

What The Live Demo Shows

A visual intuition layer before the analysis tables and results.

True world view

The left view shows the simulated truth state. It is analyst-facing only, so that a viewer can compare what is really happening with what the radar and tracker manage to reconstruct.

Radar sensing view

The radar view is ego-centric. Measurements are noisy and only appear when the rotating beam revisits an object inside the accessible sensing geometry.

Estimated tracks

The tracker fuses repeated radar updates into an estimated state. When revisit is fast and motion stays close to constant velocity, the estimate remains tight; otherwise it starts to lag or drift.

Core Concepts

The minimum concepts needed to understand the app without reading code.

Radar measurement

A scan gives imperfect range and bearing, not direct access to the full true state. The tracker only improves when new measurements arrive.

Radar revisit

Revisit rate controls how often an object is seen again. Faster revisit means more frequent corrections and less time for prediction error to grow.

Kalman constant-velocity model

The estimator assumes motion is locally first-order. That is simple, fast, and often useful, but it becomes fragile when truth dynamics contain stronger curvature or acceleration.

Truth-model mismatch

The simulated objects can follow bounded second-order motion. This mismatch is intentional: it lets us test how robust the tracker remains when the underlying motion is not perfectly aligned with the estimator model.

What This Demonstrates

The intended engineering message, in plain language.

A tracking model can be useful before it is perfect

The app shows why a simple tracking model may still be operationally helpful, but also why sensing cadence, sensing precision, and motion mismatch matter. In other words: a decent estimator can support proximity operations, but only inside a sensing-and-dynamics envelope that should be made explicit.

Limits Of The Sandbox

Useful caveats before over-interpreting the results.

  • The truth model is physically inspired, not a full orbital mechanics model.
  • The radar is local and proximity-oriented, not a representation of large-scale ground surveillance.
  • The tracker is deliberately simple, so that estimator mismatch remains visible.
  • The point is comparative insight, not flight qualification.
